# TrackBus v0.2 experiment report

## Scope and evidence level

The final bounded v0.2 matrix was run on `data/input/test_video.mp4` after the
implementation and fusion behavior were frozen:

- source: 320 x 240, 25 FPS, 589 frames;
- input SHA-256:
  `2f31908930d306d3bd07b16c9b26881984107f3eb235f3a13b514de0077a719b`;
- aggregate ground truth: 6 IN, 2 OUT;
- Python 3.14.3 on Windows 11;
- Ultralytics 8.4.95 and Torch 2.13.0 CPU-only;
- tracker profile: `configs/bytetrack_trackbus.yaml`;
- fusion: conservative class-aware cross-view NMS at IoU 0.50, maximum
  confidence;
- counting state machine: unchanged from v0.1.2.

No frame-level ground-truth annotation file existed. Precision, recall, F1,
false-positive events, false-negative events, and timing error therefore cannot
be calculated. The ranking is aggregate-only and uses direction count error,
diagnostic false-event risk, then FPS. Matching aggregate totals would not by
itself prove that the correct events were found.

Command:

```powershell
python -m trackbus.experiments --input data/input/test_video.mp4 --matrix configs/experiments/test_video.yaml --output-dir data/experiments/test_video_v020_final
```

Generated experiment media, resolved configs, and machine-local paths remain
ignored by Git. The committed matrix and this report contain no absolute local
path.

## Baseline safeguard

The preserved v0.1.2 summary reported 3 IN, 2 OUT, 250 tracked observations, 15
unique IDs, and 24.43 FPS. Configuration A through the new explicit pipeline
again produced 3 IN, 2 OUT, 250 tracked observations, and 15 IDs. This reproduces
the baseline counting/tracking result while changing the detector/tracker
architecture.

The historical 24.43 FPS was not reproducible in the current dependency/runtime
state. A controlled current-environment run of the retained combined
`YOLO.track` path achieved 10.74 FPS with full video writing and diagnostics; the
final v0.2 explicit A run achieved 8.65 FPS. A detector/tracker-only legacy loop
measured 9.82 FPS. Thus the saved-to-current drop must not all be attributed to
multi-view or fusion, while the explicit full-frame path was about 19% slower
than the apples-to-apples current legacy full pipeline. Performance remains a
known optimization target.

## Detection coverage

Lower confidence and the small model increased detector output even without
multiple views. Relative to B, D's overlapping nano views increased raw
detections from 415 to 1,170 and reduced raw-empty frames from 350 to 236. Fusion
reduced the pooled source-coordinate set from 1,170 to 865, removing 305
cross-view duplicate or ambiguous spanning hypotheses.

Relative to C, E increased raw detections from 778 to 1,613 and reduced raw-empty
frames from 216 to 152. Fusion reduced 1,613 hypotheses to 1,179, a reduction of
434. Multi-view therefore genuinely improved detector coverage on this clip in
the narrow sense of more person hypotheses and fewer frames with no hypothesis.

There are no box-level human labels, so the additional detections cannot be
classified as true people versus door/static false positives. `likely_static`
was zero for all runs, but that heuristic is not a false-positive measurement.

## Tracking and event outcome

The extra detections did not become additional complete, reliable crossing
histories:

- D changed B's 3 IN / 2 OUT to 3 IN / 3 OUT, increasing direction count error
  from 3 to 4;
- E changed C's 3 IN / 2 OUT to 2 IN / 2 OUT, also increasing direction count
  error from 3 to 4;
- D expanded from B's 18 IDs and 1 restart warning to 42 IDs and 7 warnings;
- E expanded from C's 21 IDs and 3 restart warnings to 36 IDs and 8 warnings.

With only eight manually verified crossings, 42 or 36 temporary IDs are strong
fragmentation/duplicate-risk indicators. They do not prove an exact number of ID
switches. D's extra OUT is an aggregate mismatch, not enough evidence to identify
a specific false-positive event; E's lower IN total likewise does not identify a
specific missed label. Frame-level annotation is required for those claims.

## Throughput trade-off

Three views require three predictions for every source frame. D fell from B's
8.18 FPS to 2.09 FPS, about a 74% reduction. E fell from C's 4.17 FPS to 1.28
FPS, about a 69% reduction. Neither multi-view configuration approached the 25
FPS source rate on CPU. Small-model multi-view took 458.56 seconds to process
23.56 seconds of video.

These measurements are environment-specific, but the relative cost is clear:
the tested multi-view configurations are not real-time CPU options.

## Conclusion

Multi-view inference helped raw and fused detection coverage but did **not**
improve passenger-event counting on this video. Both matched comparisons had a
worse aggregate direction error, substantially more IDs and restart warnings,
and much lower CPU throughput. Configuration A remains the aggregate-only leader
because it has the lowest direction error, the lowest diagnostic risk, and the
simplest/faster pipeline. This is not an event-accuracy claim.

The experiment supports keeping the conservative counter unchanged. The next
evidence step is human frame-level event annotation, followed by evaluation on
representative buses and door cycles. Given the observed blinking boxes, merged
people, overhead viewpoint, door false positives, and the failure of additional
generic-model views to improve complete events, a properly licensed custom
overhead-head or overhead-person detector is now recommended for evaluation. It
should plug into the model-independent detector interface and be tested on
held-out cameras rather than tuned to this one clip.
